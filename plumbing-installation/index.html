Permits and licenses: what Blue Island asks for

Blue Island code section 150.052 requires a plumbing permit for plumbing installation and repair. The application goes to the city's Building Safety Department, and the Plumbing Inspector verifies that the applicant holds a valid plumbing license or is the owner-occupant of a single-family residence. The permit is presented to the Department of Water and Sewer before work begins.

A licensed plumber applies as the owner's agent, and the code requires the contractor to call the Plumbing Inspector before pipes are covered. Final water start follows inspection. Ask Building Safety at City Hall, 13051 Greenwood Avenue, about your exact job before you buy materials, since a small change in scope can change what the permit covers.

State law backs this up. The Illinois Plumbing License Law, 225 ILCS 320, requires municipal plumbing inspectors to be licensed plumbers and bars permits without license verification. You can check any plumber on the Illinois Department of Public Health plumbing license lookup.

From first call to final inspection

Planned plumbing runs in a set order. Skipping a stage is how a rough-in ends up behind drywall before anyone checks it.

Walk-through and scope

We look at the space, the nearest supply and drain lines, and where vents will run, then write the price before work starts.

02

Permit and scheduling

The permit goes in through Building Safety, and any utility locates are scheduled if digging is involved.

03

Rough-in

Supply, drain and vent lines are installed and pressure tested, and the Plumbing Inspector is called before anything is covered.

04

Fixtures and final

Fixtures are set and connected, the system is tested again, and the inspector signs off before final water start.

Digging for a new line? Call 811 first

A new water service, sewer lateral or yard line needs a utility locate before a shovel goes in. In Illinois you call 811 (JULIE), and the excavator makes the call, not the homeowner. JULIE asks for at least two working days notice, and the tolerance zone is 18 inches on each side of a marked line, where digging must be by hand or vacuum.

The Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, puts the water service pipe at least 36 inches deep or below the frost line, whichever is deeper. Plan the trench and the locate dates together, or the job stalls.

Tying new plumbing into Blue Island's older homes

About 64.7% of Blue Island housing was built before 1970, and the median home dates to 1961. New fixtures in homes that old may connect to iron, galvanized or mixed older drain and supply lines. We check what the new work will tie into before quoting, because connecting copper or PEX to corroded galvanized can fail at the joint.

Each main building in Blue Island needs its own water service pipe, tap and meter, with a shutoff just ahead of the meter, under city code section 52.009. That matters for additions and detached units. A new building cannot simply borrow the main house's line.

In the Olde Western Avenue Historic District or on a landmarked property, exterior work can need a Certificate of Appropriateness. Call Building Safety before planning visible changes.

New fixtures, lines and appliances are what Blue Island code section 150.052 calls plumbing installation, so a permit comes first. The Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890) governs the work. The plumber must also call the city's plumbing inspector before any pipe is covered.

New-construction and addition work follows the same permit path as repairs: a licensed plumber pulls the plumbing permit under section 150.052, and the inspector must see rough-in pipes before they are covered. Plan your plumbing rough-in dates around that inspection, because a covered pipe that has not been seen has to be opened up again.

Before you hire for plumbing installation

City code section 150.052 requires permits for plumbing installation and repair, applied for at the Building Safety Department by a plumber as the owner's agent. The inspector checks for a valid license or owner-occupant status.

For a small fixture swap, ask Building Safety at City Hall before you assume either way.

The code lets an owner-occupant of a single-family residence apply for the permit, but the work still has to pass inspection. Many owners hire a licensed plumber for rough-in and leave finish work to themselves.

Use the Illinois Department of Public Health plumbing license lookup. Plumbing contractors working in Blue Island must also be registered with the city, and the city asks for the state IDPH licenses on file.
