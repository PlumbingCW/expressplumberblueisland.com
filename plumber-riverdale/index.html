Riverdale sits between Harvey, Dolton and Blue Island on 3.7 square miles of very flat ground, with the Little Calumet River along its northern edge. What happens to water here depends on which part of the village you live in. Below we go neighbourhood by neighbourhood, from the northeast side to the Metra stops, and say which problem each tends to bring.

The northeast side and the blocks north of 138th Street

The county hazard plan says areas north of 138th Street and east of School Street have flooding issues, with seepage and sewage in basements. It also says homes on the northeast side of town have experienced sewage backups, and that a sewer separation project there has prevented backups from heavy rain.

The village secured $7,393,575 in CDBG-DR funds in 2016 for northeast sewer separation and flood relief. If you live in that quadrant and still see water in the basement, a camera run on your own sewer line tells you whether the break is in your lateral or beyond it, and that decides who pays.

A flat village on combined and separate sewers

The Metropolitan Water Reclamation District lists Riverdale as part combined and part separate sewers, and as a member of the Calumet Tunnel and Reservoir Plan. That plan has 36.7 miles of tunnels and the Thornton Reservoir, which holds 4.8 billion gallons and was completed in 2015.

The Center for Neighborhood Technology measured a slope of only about 0.15% across the village. Water drains slowly on ground that flat, so a sump discharge pipe that dumps on the lawn can pool and soak back toward the house. Its guide also recommends televising and cleaning about 10% of the village's sewers each year, and a sewer inspection on your own line follows the same logic.

Midlothian adopted its own RainReady plan first, while Alsip sits only on separate sewers. Neither is a safe guide to your street.

Homes built before 1970, and what insurers have paid

Riverdale's median home dates to 1957, 59% were built between 1940 and 1969, and nearly 15% before 1940. Older laterals from that period often have joints that roots find and shapes that settle in soft, flat soil. About half of the units are detached houses, and 3-4 unit buildings add roughly 8% more.

CNT counted 3,362 flood-related insurance claims in ZIP 60827 between 2007 and 2011, with more than $8 million paid. Sudden damage is the usual covered case, while gradual seepage, wear and sewer backup often need a specific endorsement, so check your policy and keep photographs. For basements that keep taking water, basement waterproofing is a planned fix, not an emergency one.

Riverdale's Inspectional Services says plumbing installations require a building permit. Plumbing contractors register with proof of a state licence, issued under the Illinois Plumbing License Law (225 ILCS 320). Ask your plumber for the permit before a Riverdale repair that opens walls or floors.

What to do before we arrive in Riverdale

Emergency dispatch for Riverdale is staffed around the clock. Backups here often involve sewage, so safety comes before cleanup.

Treat backup water as dirty

Keep children, pets and bare hands out of it. Wear boots if you must cross the floor.

Stop the load

No flushing, laundry or showers until the line is cleared. Every flush adds to the backup.

Switch off power to wet areas

Only from a dry spot at the panel. If you cannot reach it dry, stay upstairs and call.

Photograph, then call

Take pictures for your insurer, then call (773) 207-0518 and tell us the street, plus if you are north of 138th.
