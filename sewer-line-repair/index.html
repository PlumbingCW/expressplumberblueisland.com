Repair is rarely one-size. A short break calls for a small dig, a long run of rough pipe may take a liner, and corroded cast iron has its own rules.

Signs the pipe is broken, not just blocked

A blockage can be cleared. A broken pipe leaves clues that return no matter how often it is cleaned.

  • 1Backups that return within weeks. The cable opens the line and debris catches again on the same spot.
  • 2A sunken or soggy strip in the yard. Soil is washing into a crack or water is escaping.
  • 3Sewer smell in the yard or crawlspace. Gas is leaking from a joint.
  • 4Slow drains in every fixture, then sudden relief. A partial collapse is trapping and releasing waste.
  • 5Fresh roots on every cable pull. Roots are entering through a break.
  • 6Camera footage showing gaps, offsets or missing pipe. This is the definitive sign.

Cast iron sewer pipe: what age does to it

About 64.7% of Blue Island housing was built before 1970 (CMAP, 2020 to 2024 survey data), and city code lists cast iron as an accepted house sewer material. Cast iron lasts for decades, but it rusts from the inside, forms rough bumps called tubercles, and eventually thins or cracks.

A scaled cast iron line catches grease and paper, so it clogs more as it ages. A heavy hammer test, camera footage and sometimes a cutaway section tell us how much wall is left. Pipe that is still thick can be descaled and kept. Pipe that crumbles needs replacing or lining.

Digging to replace a house sewer starts with a JULIE 811 ticket filed by the excavator. JULIE asks for at least two business days of notice and hand digging within 18 inches of marked lines. Blue Island code section 150.052 also requires the plumbing inspector to be called before pipe is covered.

Pipe lining, explained plainly

Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. A felt tube soaked in epoxy resin is pulled or inverted into the cleaned line, inflated against the walls and left to harden. The result is a smooth, joint-free pipe that seals cracks and shuts out roots.

It has limits. The line has to be cleaned and camera-checked first, the new pipe is a little narrower, and side connections must be cut open afterward. A pipe that has collapsed or sags and holds water cannot be lined.

Callers often ask about epoxy lining under a basement slab. It can work through a cleanout, but only if the camera shows the pipe is sound enough, and we will say so honestly.

How a dig repair runs, from call to backfill

Excavation has rules, and a licensed plumber follows them.

01

Camera and locate

We map where the pipe breaks and how deep it sits, and mark the route at the surface.

02

811 and permit

Illinois law requires the excavator to call JULIE at 811, with at least two working days of notice. Blue Island code also calls for a plumbing permit, applied for by a licensed plumber.

03

Dig and replace

Utilities within 18 inches of a marked line are exposed by hand. We cut out bad pipe and set new pipe at the correct slope.

04

Inspect, then cover

The city plumbing inspector must be called before pipe is covered. After that we backfill and test with water.

Combined sewers and why a repair is only part of the fix

Most of Blue Island drains through a combined sewer that carries stormwater and sewage together. Three overflow points discharge into the Cal-Sag Channel, at Irving Avenue and two at Division Street. The county flood plan blames the age and capacity of the system for flooding in low spots.

A repaired house sewer fixes your pipe, not the street. If your basement fills in heavy rain even after a repair, ask us about a backwater valve or an overhead sewer, which are the options neighborhood flood groups suggest. For historic buildings near Western Avenue, ask Building Safety about review before exterior digging.
