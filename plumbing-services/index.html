Emergency or planned: how we sort the work

Urgent jobs get the 24/7 line. Planned jobs get a scheduled visit and time to compare options. The difference saves you money and stress.

Urgent, call now

Burst pipes, sewage backup, active leaks, a flooded basement, a gas smell (call the gas utility or 911 first).

Same-day appointment when available

Dead water heaters with no leak, single toilets that will not flush, failing sump pumps before a storm.

Planned work

Tankless upgrades, repiping, new fixtures, sewer inspections and waterproofing, where the best result comes from deciding with time.

Why older Blue Island homes show up in these lists

About 64.7% of Blue Island's housing was built before 1970, and the median build year is 1961. That means cast iron drains, older galvanized supply lines and original shutoff valves are common. They can still work, but they fail more often than newer materials.

Most of the city also uses a combined sewer system that carries sewage and rain together. During heavy storms, that is why backups and sump problems rise. Your best move is to know which services fit before the storm arrives.

Which rule applies depends on the job. Blue Island code section 150.052 covers permits for plumbing installation and repair, section 52.009 covers house sewers and water services, and the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890) is the state code. Ask your plumber which one touches your project.

What a visit usually looks like

A plumber arrives, listens to your description and looks at the fixture, the pipe run and the shutoff. The plumber then explains the cause, the options and a clear price. Only after you agree does the repair begin.

Afterward the plumber runs water through the fix, checks for drips and shows you what changed. If something else is near failure, you are told about it, and you decide whether to schedule it.

How to choose between a repair and a replacement

Ask the plumber three questions: what failed, what will fail next, and what does each option cost. A repair makes sense when one part has worn out and the rest is sound. Replacement makes sense when the same system fails twice or the repair cost is close to the price of a new unit.
