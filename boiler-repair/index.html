Smell gas? Leave before you call anyone

The rotten-egg odor added to natural gas means a possible leak. Do not flip switches, use a phone inside, or light anything. Walk out, leave the door open if you can do so safely, and call the gas utility from outside. Nicor Gas lists a 24-hour emergency and leak line on its contact page, and 911 is always right.

Signs your boiler or radiators need attention

Boilers usually announce trouble before they quit.

  • 1Some radiators stay cold. Trapped air, a closed valve or a blocked pipe may be to blame.
  • 2The pressure gauge reads too low or too high. Either one can shut a boiler down or trigger the relief valve.
  • 3Banging, gurgling or kettle-like noises. These point to air, scale or low water levels.
  • 4Water on the floor around the boiler. A pinhole in a fitting or a weeping relief valve can cause it.
  • 5Heat that cycles on and off. A faulty thermostat, pump or sensor is common.
  • 6Rusty or discolored water at a bleeder. That means corrosion inside the loop.

Parts that give boilers trouble

These are the usual suspects in a no-heat or low-heat call.

Pressure relief valve

A safety valve that opens to release excess pressure. A valve that drips or discharges should be inspected, never capped.

Circulator pump

It pushes hot water through the loop. A failed pump leaves the boiler hot and the radiators cold.

Expansion tank

A waterlogged tank causes pressure swings and repeated valve discharge.

Zone valves and radiator valves

Stuck valves block flow to a room or an entire zone.

Cold weather and why timing matters

The NWS Chicago-area (O'Hare) January average low is 18.8 F, and February averages 21.8 F. A boiler that fails then can drain heat from a house within hours, and exposed pipes in a cold basement can freeze. If your heat fails, open cabinet doors under sinks and keep a tap dripping on exposed pipes.
