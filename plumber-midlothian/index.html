Spring thaw and April storms: test the pump before it matters

A federal flood declaration followed the storms of April 2013 (DR-4116), and the county hazard plan lists six FEMA repetitive-loss properties in Midlothian, every one of them a single-family house. Midlothian adopted a RainReady plan in January 2016, and the Center for Neighborhood Technology describes chronic flooding here even in moderately sized rainstorms. Riverdale later built its own RainReady guide.

About 74% of Midlothian's homes are detached houses, so most basements have a pit, a pump or both. Pour a bucket of water into the pit in early spring. If the pump does not start, hums without moving water or cycles again and again, it needs sump pump repair before the next storm, not after.

Summer cloudbursts and the creek corridors

Midlothian has named creek trouble spots. Natalie Creek runs through the 147th to 149th Street stretch, and Midlothian Creek floods between 149th Street and Waverly Avenue, from Keeler to Kenton. A tributary creek from LaVergne to Central, between 145th Street and Midlothian Turnpike, is flagged in the hazard plan as a place where emergency vehicles and residents cannot pass.

Older blocks, 147th Street and what drains do there

Midlothian's median home dates to 1965, about half were built between 1940 and 1969 and one in ten before 1940. Older drain lines tend to have offset joints and root entry, which is why a basement drain can back up even though the house is dry.

The village is televising, lining and repairing manholes in its own sanitary sewers to cut inflow, and a camera in your lateral shows whether your pipe or the main is at fault.

That is where drain cleaning with a cable and a camera pays off. The 147th Street corridor, near the Metra station at 3750 W 147th, is the focus of the village's stormwater and street plan, which exists because of that chronic flooding.

Midlothian's plumbing permit requirements ask for an application, a letter of intent, and a scope with drawings and materials. They call for at least two inspections, rough and final, with a call 24 hours ahead. The Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890) is the state code behind that review.

Permits and inspections for new plumbing

The village requires a permit for new or relocated plumbing, and contractors must be licensed with the village each year. The plumbing requirements sheet asks for a scope with drawings and materials, then a rough and a final inspection, with 24 hours' notice to the building department.

We did not find a village rule on sump pump discharge or backflow devices, so ask the building department before you re-route a discharge line or add an ejector pit.

Before we reach your Midlothian basement

Dispatch for Midlothian runs around the clock. These four moves cost nothing and cut the damage.

Stay out of standing water

If the basement floor is wet, turn off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry. Otherwise stay upstairs and call.

Lift valuables

Move boxes, rugs and furnace-room items off the floor to dry shelves.

Check the pump outlet

Look outside for the discharge pipe. Clear leaves or ice from the end and make sure it points away from the house.

Shut off water for a burst

Close the main valve near the meter, open the lowest tap to drain the line and dial (773) 207-0518.
