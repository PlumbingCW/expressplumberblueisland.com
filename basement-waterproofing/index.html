Basement Waterproofing in Blue Island, IL

A wet basement has more than one cause. We handle the plumbing side, and we say so honestly when the fix belongs to a foundation or waterproofing contractor.

Basement waterproofing sounds like one job, but it covers at least three different problems: water seeping through walls and floors, sewer water backing up through drains, and plumbing leaks inside the house. Each one needs a different fix, and paying for the wrong one wastes money. We start by working out which problem you have.

Blue Island has older housing and a combined sewer system, so wet basements are a regular topic here. Our licensed plumbers install interior drain tile, sump pits and backwater valves, and we tell you plainly when a waterproofing contractor or foundation specialist should take over. Find where the water comes from before spending anything

Dry the floor, then watch where the damp returns. A line along the wall-floor joint points to groundwater pressing through the foundation. Water rising through a floor drain after rain points to the sewer, and a wet spot under a pipe or the water heater points to a leak.

Note the timing too. Water that shows up only during or after heavy rain behaves like a drainage or sewer problem. Water that appears on dry days is more likely a supply or drain leak, and our leak detection work covers that case.

The plumbing pieces of a dry-basement plan

Interior systems work by giving water an easy path to a pit, then pumping it out. Here is what we install or repair.

Interior drain tile

Perforated pipe along the inside of the footing, set in gravel and covered by new concrete, directs groundwater to the pit.

Sump pit and pump

A lined pit collects the water and a pump sends it out through a discharge line. See sump pump installation and repair for the pump itself.

Backwater valve or check valve

A valve in the sewer line closes when sewage tries to flow backward, so a surcharged sewer cannot push water into the floor drain.

Floor drain and sewer line work

Roots, grease and old clay pipe slow drainage. Cleaning or repairing the line removes one cause of backups.

What a plumber cannot promise

We cannot waterproof a foundation wall from the outside, repair structural cracks, or regrade your yard. We will not guarantee that a basement stays dry in every storm, because a flooded street can overwhelm any home's drainage.

What we can do is fix the plumbing causes, install the drainage equipment, and tell you what is left. If a waterproofing contractor or structural engineer is the right next call, we say so before you spend more.

Blue Island's older housing and the combined sewer

About 64.7% of Blue Island homes were built before 1970, and the median build year is 1961. Older foundations tend to be block or poured concrete with clay or cast iron drain lines, so both seepage and sewer issues are common questions. That is why we check the floor drain and main line, not only the walls.

Most of the city sits on a combined sewer system. Washington Street and Vermont Street near California are named as low-lying areas where the county's hazard plan reports urban flooding. A nonprofit study of the Calumet Corridor recommended check valves and overhead sewers for homes in this kind of area.

A sump pit tied to a new discharge line counts as plumbing installation, and Blue Island code section 150.052 requires a permit for that work. The Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890) sets the plumbing standards behind it. Discharge rules for sump pumps vary by municipality, so ask Building Safety where your pump may drain.

How an interior drain tile and sump job works

This is a planned project, scheduled around your home rather than a storm.

01

Inspect and plan

We check walls, floor drains and the sewer line, then explain the cause and give you a clear price before work starts.

02

Open the floor

A saw cuts a strip of concrete along the wall, and we dig a trench down to the footing.

03

Lay drain tile and gravel

Perforated pipe slopes toward the pit and is covered with washed stone.

04

Set the pit and pump

The pit is sunk below floor level, lined, fitted with a pump and connected to the discharge line.

05

Pour and test

New concrete closes the trench. We then pour water in and watch the pump clear it.

What to note before we visit

A few details save time at the first appointment.

  • 1Where the water appears. Walls, floor cracks, the floor drain or around a pipe.
  • 2When it appears. During rain, days after rain, or on dry days.
  • 3What is on the floor. Stored items, finished walls and carpet affect access and moisture checks.
  • 4Past work. Any earlier waterproofing, sump pit or sewer repair, and photos if you have them.
  • 5Your insurance policy. Check whether sewer backup coverage is listed.
