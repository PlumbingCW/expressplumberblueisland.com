Plumbing emergency at your business after hours? Protect the building first

The first ten minutes decide how much stock, flooring and equipment you lose. Act on the building before you act on the problem, and call from a dry, safe spot.

  • Find and close the main water shutoff. City code requires a shutoff just ahead of the water meter, so check there first and mark the valve for staff.
  • Switch off power to wet areas and equipment on the floor. Do not step into standing water near outlets, coolers or fryer lines.
  • Stop using sinks, toilets and floor drains if sewage is coming up. Block off the area so customers and staff stay out.
  • Move stock, paper goods and electronics above the waterline, and photograph the damage for your insurer.
  • Call (773) 207-0518 and tell us the address, what failed, and where the water is going.

The City Water and Sewer Department keeps weekday hours only, 7:30 am to 5 pm, so a night or weekend break is yours to contain until a plumber arrives. That is why this line stays open around the clock.

Commercial leaks: what we can find, and where we say so

A rising water bill is the most common sign of a hidden commercial leak. Blue Island reads commercial meters and bills them monthly, so a jump shows up within weeks, unlike a home that is read every other month. Check the meter with every fixture off. If the dial moves, water is leaking somewhere.

We use listening equipment, thermal imaging and pressure tests to narrow the location. Not every leak can be pinpointed without opening a wall, floor or slab. When that is the case, we say so, give you the options and the price, and let you decide before any cutting starts.

Blue Island code section 52.009 gives each main building its own water service pipe, tap and meter, with a shutoff just ahead of the meter. Section 52.020 reads and bills commercial meters monthly. If you share a building with other tenants, ask the Water and Sewer Department which meter serves your space before any repair.

Backflow testing: what to ask before you book

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination. Test requirements come from your water supplier, not from us.

  • 1Who asked for the test. If you received a notice, keep it. Ask the Blue Island Water and Sewer Department what applies to your line, since we cannot state a rule the city has not posted.
  • 2Which assembly it is. Irrigation, fire, boiler and process lines may each carry a different assembly. Tell us what you have when you call.
  • 3Whether repairs are likely. A failed test means a repair, a retest and the paperwork. Ask us what each step involves before the visit.
  • 4Who files the result. Confirm who submits the report so your account does not get flagged for missing paperwork.

Licensing and contractor registration in Blue Island

Plumbing in Illinois is licensed by the state under the Illinois Plumbing License Law, 225 ILCS 320. You can check any plumber on the Illinois Department of Public Health plumbing license lookup. Our plumbers are licensed and insured.

Blue Island also requires contractors to register with the city under Ordinance 99-247. For plumbing contractors, the city asks for the state IDPH licenses on file, and city code sets minimum liability insurance for registered contractors. Landlords and property managers should ask to see proof before signing a work order.
