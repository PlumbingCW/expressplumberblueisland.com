Buying a water softener before you know your water is the most common mistake we hear about. Chalky glassware, spotted fixtures and scale in the kettle suggest hard water, but a test tells you how hard. Blue Island buys its water from Chicago, so the water quality report from the city is the right first stop.

Once you know what you are treating, our licensed plumbers handle the plumbing side: installing a softener or filter, repairing a leaking valve, and replacing an old unit. This is planned work, and you can book a same-day appointment when available by calling (773) 207-0518.

Test your tap before you choose equipment

We have not verified a hardness figure for Blue Island, so we will not guess one. Here is how to get your own number.

01

Ask the utility

Call the Blue Island Water and Sewer Department at City Hall, 13051 Greenwood Avenue, and ask whether the annual water quality report lists hardness.

02

Test at home

Use a test strip kit or a lab test from a certified laboratory, drawn from a cold tap that has not been softened.

03

Read the units

Hardness is reported as grains per gallon or milligrams per liter. Mixing them up leads to oversized equipment.

04

Match the equipment

A softener removes scale-forming minerals. A filter targets other things, such as taste or particles. Many homes need only one.

What the 2025 Blue Island water report says, and what it does not

Blue Island's 2025 Consumer Confidence Report shows water that comes from Lake Michigan, treated at Chicago's Jardine and Sawyer plants. The city states it met or exceeded all drinking water standards. The report lists a highest fluoride reading of 0.75 ppm and sodium up to 9.10 ppm, but we could not find a hardness figure in it.

That sodium number matters if you are considering a softener. Softening exchanges hardness minerals for sodium or potassium, so ask your doctor if you are on a low-sodium diet. Never treat the report as a hardness test.

A filter makes no claim about hardness, and a softener makes no claim about lead. Buying the wrong one is easy, which is why we start with the test.

Older pipes and lead: a different question from hardness

Blue Island's service line inventory, dated April 1, 2025, lists 5,433 total lines. Of those, 529 are lead, 81 are galvanized and need replacement, and 4,707 are still unknown. In August 2026 the city offered free in-home service line identification, and you can ask the Water and Sewer Department whether it is still available.

A softener does not remove lead. If you learn your line is lead, a plumber can discuss replacement and the city can advise on testing. Until then, ask about a filter independently certified for lead reduction. Our repiping and water line repair pages cover work on the pipes themselves.

What we install and repair

Our work covers the plumbing connections and the common failure points.

Softener installation

Bypass valve, hard-water supply, drain line for the brine discharge and a nearby outlet for the control head.

Filter installation

Whole-house or under-sink housings, with a shutoff and a pressure gauge where it makes sense.

Valve and head repair

Broken or leaking valves, cracked fittings and damaged lines, including those hit during moves or construction.

Replacement

We disconnect the old tank, size the new one and test for leaks.

Problems we hear about softener systems

These are the situations that prompt people to call.

  • 1Salt level does not drop. The unit may not be regenerating or the brine tank may have a bridge.
  • 2Water is still hard. The resin may be spent, the bypass may be open or the control head may be stuck.
  • 3Leaking at the valve. Damage from a move or construction sometimes cracks a tank or fitting.
  • 4Brine drain overflow. A kinked drain line or a clogged air gap can send water to the floor.
  • 5Odd noises during regeneration. That can be normal, but constant running is not.

Where the equipment goes in a Blue Island home

Most softeners go in the basement near the main shutoff and a floor drain. The City of Blue Island's code requires each main building to have its own water service pipe, tap, meter and a shutoff ahead of the meter, which is where your main line enters.

Permit questions go to the Building Safety Department at City Hall, which can say whether a softener hookup needs one. For fixtures that need work alongside the softener, see faucet repair.

Installing a softener or filter ties into your supply piping, so it is plumbing work under the Illinois Plumbing License Law (225 ILCS 320) and the Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890. Ask any installer to show a current state licence, and check it against the Illinois Department of Public Health licence lookup before signing.

If you already own a softener or conditioner that has stopped working, the usual repair calls are a stuck or noisy control head, a brine tank that never draws, a drain line that has kinked or frozen, and a resin tank that no longer softens. Describe which one you see when you call (773) 207-0518, because the fix for a bad control valve is very different from the fix for exhausted resin.
