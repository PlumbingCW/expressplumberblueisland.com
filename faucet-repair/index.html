Which faucet or valve is giving you trouble?

The fix depends on the fixture. Each type wears out in its own way, so name yours when you call.

Kitchen faucet

Pull-down hoses crack, spray heads clog and single-handle cartridges wear out. We repair most models or fit a new one.

Bathroom sink faucet

Two-handle faucets drip from worn washers or seats. Single-handle models usually need a new cartridge.

Bathtub faucet and spout

A tub that keeps running after the handle is off often has a worn stem, seat or diverter. The spout may also be loose against the wall.

Shower valve

The valve sits behind the wall tile. Temperature swings and a trickle after shutoff usually mean a failing pressure-balance cartridge.

Outdoor spigot

Hose bibs split in freezing weather and leak inside the wall. Tell us if you see a wet spot inside near the spigot.

Valve will not shut off? Stop the water in four moves

A fixture valve that fails open can pour water onto a floor all night. Do not keep twisting the handle, since that can strip the stem and make the repair bigger.

  • Look under the sink for the small shutoff valves on the hot and cold lines and turn each clockwise until it stops.
  • For a tub or shower with no shutoff nearby, close the main water valve where the line enters your home, often near the meter in a basement or utility room.
  • Open one other faucet low in the house to drain the pressure, then check the failed fixture to see if the flow has stopped.
  • Keep the area clear of electrical cords and stay out of rooms where water has reached outlets. Then call (773) 207-0518.

Evenings, weekends and holidays are normal hours for this call. Our after-hours line is the same 24/7 line, and a same-day appointment when available can handle a faucet that is dripping but contained.

What Blue Island water and older homes do to faucets

Blue Island buys water from Chicago, which draws it from Lake Michigan. The city's water report does not state a hardness figure, so test your tap instead of guessing. White crust on the aerator or showerhead is a sign that minerals are building up in the valve too.

About 64.7% of local housing was built before 1970, with a median build year of 1961. Faucets and tub valves in those homes are often decades old, and replacement parts for discontinued models can be hard to find. In that case a new faucet or valve body is usually cheaper than chasing old parts.

Winters matter for outdoor spigots. Chicago-area January lows average about 18.8 degrees Fahrenheit at O'Hare, and a spigot in an uninsulated wall can freeze and split. Shut off and drain the line before the first hard freeze.

Blue Island code section 150.052 requires a permit for plumbing installation and repair, and the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890) sets the technical rules. Whether a cartridge swap counts is a question for Building Safety, so ask before assuming either way.

How we repair a faucet or shower valve

The method is simple, and you can watch each part.

01

Isolate the fixture

We close the supply stops under the sink or the main valve for a wall valve, then relieve pressure at the spout.

02

Remove the handle and trim

The cartridge, stem or seat comes out so we can see the real damage instead of guessing from the drip.

03

Replace worn parts or the fixture

A cartridge, seat, O-ring or stem is replaced with the matching part, or we fit a new faucet if parts are gone.

04

Test hot, cold and shutoff

We run both temperatures, check for leaks under the sink and behind the wall plate, and confirm the handle stops the flow cleanly.

Signs a faucet or valve is close to failing

Catch these early and the repair stays small.

  • 1A slow drip that never stops. One drip per second adds up to a noticeable amount of wasted water over a month.
  • 2A stiff or gritty handle. Mineral buildup and a worn cartridge fight your hand before they fail outright.
  • 3Water behind the shower wall. A stained ceiling below, or soft drywall next to the valve, points to a leak at the valve body.
  • 4Pressure that drops at one fixture only. That usually means a clogged aerator or cartridge. If every fixture is weak, ask about a water line repair check instead.

What to do before we arrive at a leaking faucet or valve

A few minutes of prep limits the damage and lets the technician start on the repair, not on clearing the cabinet.

  • 1Close the fixture stop. Turn the small valve under the sink clockwise. If it spins loose or leaks, close the main shutoff instead.
  • 2Clear the cabinet. Empty the space under the sink and set a bucket under the drip so the valves are easy to reach.
  • 3Dry the area. Wipe standing water off the floor and counter so nobody slips and the cabinet base does not soak.
  • 4Keep electricity away from water. Do not touch outlets, cords or appliances near a wet spot. Stay out of the room if water reaches an outlet.
  • 5Note what you see. Write down the faucet brand if it is printed on the body, and take a photo of where the water escapes.
