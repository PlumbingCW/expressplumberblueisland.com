Pipe just burst: your first ten minutes

Find the main shutoff, often by the meter, and turn it fully. Then open the lowest faucet in the house to drain what is left in the lines.

Stay away from standing water near outlets, cords and the electrical panel. If the ceiling is sagging with trapped water, keep out from underneath it.

  • Close the main shutoff and drain the lowest tap.
  • Switch off power to wet areas only if you can reach the breaker while dry.
  • Move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the water's path.
  • Photograph damage before cleanup and call (773) 207-0518.

What each pipe material does when it fails

The material in your walls changes the repair. A quick look at the pipe tells us what we are dealing with.

Copper

Freeze damage shows as a lengthwise split. Corrosion shows as pinholes with green crust. Both are repaired by cutting out the damaged section and joining new copper.

Galvanized steel

Rust builds inside the pipe and weakens threaded joints. One repair often reveals more thin spots nearby, so replacement of the run is worth pricing.

Cast iron

Used mostly for drains and stacks, it cracks or corrodes through rather than bursting under pressure. Cracks show up as seepage, odor or staining below.

Plastic (PEX, CPVC, PVC)

Cracks often trace back to freezing, a bad fitting or impact. We replace the section and check the fittings around it.

Frozen pipe repair: thaw, then inspect

A frozen pipe may not have split yet. If a faucet gives only a trickle in cold weather, keep it open, close the main if you see any wet spot, and call (773) 207-0518. Do not use an open flame on a pipe.

Chicago-area (O'Hare) normals show January averaging a low of 18.8 F and February 21.8 F, with about 38.4 inches of snow a year. Those temperatures put unheated crawlspaces, garages and exterior-wall runs at risk.

We thaw the pipe slowly, then check joints and the pipe wall for splits before the water goes back on. The ice often hides the break, and the leak starts only as it melts.

Why Blue Island pipes fail in winter

About 64.7% of Blue Island homes were built before 1970, with a median build year of 1961. Older pipe walls are often thinner from corrosion, so a hard freeze finds the weak spot.

The Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890.1150) requires the water service pipe to be buried at least 36 inches, or at frost depth if deeper. A service line shallower than that is a freeze risk, so tell us if your line was ever repaired or relocated.

The Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890.1150) requires exterior-wall pipe to be protected from freezing. A split pipe on a cold wall suggests that protection failed or never existed. Ask us to check the whole run when we repair it, so the same wall does not burst again.
