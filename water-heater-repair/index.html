Most failures sit in a short list. A dead pilot or igniter, a tripped limit switch, a burned-out element, a worn thermostat, or a weeping fitting. A few are final, like a tank that has rusted through. The sections below sort your symptoms so you know which group you are in before anyone arrives.

Leaking tank or no hot water right now? Do this while you wait

Water on the floor comes first. Close the cold-water supply valve on the pipe feeding the top of the tank, then cut the power or fuel. If the water is creeping toward a floor drain, an outlet or the furnace, lift anything valuable out of its path.

A drip from a pipe fitting is a smaller job than a leak from the tank shell, but you cannot tell from across the basement. Take a photo for the technician and keep the area around the heater clear.

  • Close the cold-water shutoff above the tank. It is usually a lever or a round wheel handle.
  • Switch off the breaker for an electric unit. For a gas unit, turn the gas control knob to off.
  • Stay out of standing water if it reaches an outlet, a cord or the electrical panel.
  • Smell gas or rotten eggs? Leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from outside. Do not touch light switches.
  • Call (773) 207-0518 and tell us the fuel type, where the tank sits and where the water is coming from.

No hot water and no leak buys you a little more time. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and the 24/7 line takes calls after hours if the heater dies on a Sunday night.

What a repair visit looks like

The order matters, because a quick guess at the wrong part costs you twice.

01

Listen and look

We ask what happened before the failure, then check the tank, the pan, the relief valve discharge pipe and the venting for water, scorch marks or rust.

02

Test fuel and power

A multimeter checks elements, thermostats and limit switches. On gas units a leak detector sweeps the joints and the burner is observed running.

03

Isolate the failed part

We confirm the fault by testing, not by swapping parts until the heat returns. You see the result before any repair starts.

04

Price, repair, retest

You get a clear price first. After the repair we run the heater through a full cycle and check for leaks, flame quality and water temperature at a tap.

Gas, electric and hybrid heat pump units fail in different ways

The fuel decides what can break, and it also decides how quickly we can usually fix it.

Gas tank

Weak flame, a sooty burner, a bad thermocouple or an igniter that clicks without lighting. Venting trouble can also shut the burner down for safety, so we check the flue after any gas repair.

Electric tank

Two heating elements and two thermostats do the work, each a common wear item. A tripped high-limit button is a symptom, so we find out why it tripped before resetting it.

Hybrid heat pump

These add a compressor, a fan, an air filter and a condensate drain. Many fall back on their electric elements when something fails, so hot water continues but the power bill climbs. Write down any error code on the display and tell us the model.

Sealed-system faults in a hybrid, such as a failed compressor, may need manufacturer parts and a longer wait. We say so plainly, and we compare that repair against a full replacement so you can choose with real numbers.

Pilot lights, thermostats and the anode rod

On an older gas heater, a small thermocouple senses the pilot flame. If it weakens, it tells the gas valve to close, and that is why a pilot dies again right after you light it. Newer gas units use an electronic igniter in its place, which can crack or wear out.

On an electric tank, the upper thermostat controls the top element and passes power to the lower one. When either thermostat drifts, you get lukewarm water or scalding spikes.

Inside the tank, a sacrificial anode rod draws corrosion away from the steel. Swapping a spent rod can extend a sound tank's life. Once the steel is pitted, no rod will save it, and we tell you so before you pay.

Repairs such as a new element, thermostat or anode rod follow the manufacturer's service instructions for your model. Plumbing-side work falls under the Illinois Plumbing License Law (225 ILCS 320). If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else.

About 64.7% of Blue Island housing was built before 1970, and the median build year is 1961. In houses that age the shutoff valve above the tank has often sat untouched for decades. It may not close fully, or it may start dripping once you turn it, so tell the technician before we reach for it. Replacing a seized valve can be part of the visit.

When repair stops making sense

A leak from the tank shell cannot be patched. The steel is gone, and the repair is a new heater, so see our water heater replacement page for that decision.

If the unit is old, has several failing parts and the tank floor is full of sediment, we will say that a repair buys you a short reprieve. Check the manufacture date on the rating plate and give us the model number when you call.
