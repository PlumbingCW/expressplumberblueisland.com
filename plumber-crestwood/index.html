Crestwood is the one village in this group that does not share a border with Blue Island, though our dispatch base at 12601 S Western Ave is only about 3 miles east-northeast of it. Most calls from here are stopped drains and leaks you cannot see. If either is happening now, the 24/7 emergency line at (773) 207-0518 is open overnight and on weekends.

The numbers explain why. Crestwood has 5,145 housing units, and only 41.2% are detached single-family houses. The rest are mostly condos and apartment buildings of five to nineteen units, and the median build year is 1976. Shared pipes, shared walls and a quarterly water bill all change how a plumber should work, so this page starts with the buildings.

Built in the 1970s and 1980s: what that means inside the walls

About 44% of Crestwood's housing went up between 1970 and 1989, another 28% between 1940 and 1969, and about 23% from 1990 to 2009. Very little is older than 1940. In homes of that age, trouble more often starts at aging joints and fittings than in the pipe itself.

Slow leaks at those joints are the hardest to catch. A pinhole behind a tub wall can run for weeks. That is where leak detection earns its keep, because acoustic listening and a thermal camera find the spot before any drywall comes down.

Condo, townhouse or detached house: who owns the problem

More than half of Crestwood's units, about 56%, sit in buildings of five or more units. That makes the first question for any drain or leak call a simple one: whose pipe is it?

What the Crestwood water record says, and what it does not

Crestwood's water department distributes Lake Michigan water purchased through the City of Chicago. The Illinois EPA's published record describes a contaminated backup well, called Well 1, that had been used to supplement that supply and was sealed in March 2009. The agency says only Lake Michigan water has been used since November 2007, and its April 2009 sampling of the distribution system found no vinyl chloride or other volatile compounds.

The village's 2025 report lists a lead 90th percentile of 2.2 parts per billion, from samples taken in July 2023, with no sites over the action level. A plumber does not test or certify drinking water. Questions about your water belong with the village water department. What we handle is the pipework inside your home.

Quarterly bills hide leaks for weeks

Crestwood bills water quarterly. Rates effective June 1, 2026 start at $7.70 per 1,000 gallons in the first tier, with a minimum sewer charge of $30 per 10,000 gallons and a capital improvement charge of $2.06 per 1,000 gallons. With a bill that arrives once a quarter, a running toilet or a slab leak can burn through most of a billing period before the number tells you.

The village's water page points to the Cook County Leak Repair program, so see whether you qualify before paying for a repair that program might cover. Either way, shut every tap, read the meter, wait twenty minutes and read it again. If the dial moved, call us and we will trace the line.

Crestwood's village page says a permit is required for installing plumbing services and that contractors must be registered. The Illinois Plumbing License Law (225 ILCS 320) is the state rule behind a plumber's licence. Ask for the licence and the permit before work on your Crestwood house begins.

Rain and creek flooding: tell dispatch your cross street

The county hazard plan ranks flood as Crestwood's number one hazard and puts most of the village in a moderate urban flooding susceptibility zone. It names repeated flooding at 135th Street and Playfield, notes that local creeks flood, and lists one FEMA repetitive-loss property. Crestwood runs on separate sewers, so the cause of a wet basement matters.

Groundwater through the slab

Damp walls and a wet floor after rain with no sewer smell. A sump pump or drainage fix is the likelier answer.

Sewer lateral blockage

Water and odour rising from the floor drain, even on a dry day. A cable or camera run is the fix.

Shared building line

Several units backing up together. That calls for the manager and a main-line clearing.

Neighbouring Midlothian has its own creek flooding along 147th Street, and Alsip has its own list of named flood spots, such as 114th and Mather. Say your cross street out loud on the phone, because it tells us which cause to check first.

How we trace a leak or clear a drain in a Crestwood building

Condo and apartment calls, whether drain cleaning or a leak hunt, follow a stricter order than a single-family visit.

01

Shut down and isolate

We close the unit shutoff, or the building valve if your manager can grant access, and note which units share the line.

02

Listen and image

Acoustic listening finds the sound of moving water. A thermal camera shows cold or warm patches behind drywall.

03

Open the smallest hole

We cut only where the readings agree, then repair the pipe or clear the drain with a cable or camera.

04

Confirm before closing

A pressure test or a flush proves the line holds, and you hear the clear price before the wall is closed.

Crestwood's building page says any plumbing installation needs a permit and that contractors must be registered with the village. Ask us about the permit step on repairs that replace more than a fitting.

What to do while you wait in Crestwood

Our emergency dispatch for Crestwood runs around the clock. These four steps matter most in a building with neighbours below you.

Close your unit shutoff

Look under the kitchen sink or inside the bath wall panel. If you live in a house, close the main near the meter.

Warn the units below

Knock or call. Water that reaches a ceiling fixture can bring the light down, so stay clear of wet electrical items.

Photograph and mop

Take a quick picture of the damage for your insurer, then move rugs and towels off the wet floor.

Reach your manager

Give them our call so the building main can be shut if needed. Then dial (773) 207-0518 and tell us the floor and unit.
