What you will find here

These guides answer one question at a time. Each starts with the short answer, then explains the reasoning, what a homeowner can safely do and when a call to a licensed plumber is smarter. We write for people standing in a wet basement, not for search engines.

Where a local fact helps, we use one with a source behind it, such as Blue Island's combined sewer system or the age of its housing. If we cannot verify a number, we leave it out.

What we cover

The guides fall into a few groups. Drain and sewer posts explain backups, slow lines and why storms push water into basements. Water heater posts cover signs a tank is failing and what to check before you replace it. Leak posts show how to read a water meter, spot hidden water and decide when to call.

We also write about planned projects, such as repiping and sump systems, where the real work happens before anyone picks up a tool. That means choosing materials, pulling permits through the city and understanding what each option costs.

How the posts are written

Every post is written for a specific question and place. There are no generic templates and no stock paragraphs that appear on every page. If a post names a cost, it explains the factors behind it instead of quoting a dollar figure that may not fit your job.

Posts also separate what is safe to try from what is not. You can plunge a toilet. You should not open a sewer cleanout in a flooded basement.

Before you open a guide in an emergency

A note on local facts

Blue Island sits on a glacial bluff south of Chicago, and its older neighborhoods around Olde Western Avenue and Uptown date from well before today's plumbing codes. That history is one reason older materials such as cast iron and galvanized steel are still in some walls. The guides keep local color short and always tie it to a decision you can make.

The Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, and the Illinois Plumbing License Law, 225 ILCS 320, are the two standards we cite most. We cite them as references for homeowners, never as proof that any one plumber is qualified. Check a licence yourself with the state lookup run by the Illinois Department of Public Health.

How we choose topics

Topics come from the problems that show up most often in older Chicago-area homes. A blocked toilet, a tank that stops making hot water and a damp basement after heavy rain all rank high. We also pick subjects where a wrong guess costs money, such as cutting into a wall before you know where a leak starts.

A good topic has a clear first step, a point where a homeowner should stop and a plain explanation of what a plumber does next. If a subject cannot meet that test, we leave it out. The result is a short list of guides that each earn their place.

How local facts are sourced

When a guide mentions Blue Island, the fact comes from a public source, such as city pages, Census data or a state agency. We do not guess at water chemistry, permit rules or utility details. If a source does not state something, the guide tells you to ask the city or test your tap instead.

Standards are named so you can look them up. The Illinois Plumbing Code is 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, and JULIE (811) handles utility locates before any digging. Use those names when you talk with a contractor or the city.

Safety first, every time

A few rules apply to almost every plumbing problem, whatever the guide says.

Gas smell

Leave the building at once and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not flip switches on the way out.

Water near electricity

Stay away from wet outlets, cords and the breaker panel. Wait for a professional if the panel is wet.

Hot water

Tank water can scald. Let a heater cool and shut off its power or fuel before opening any part.

Sewage

Backup water carries bacteria. Keep children and pets away, wear gloves and call for help with the line.
