Tankless Water Heater Not Working in Blue Island, IL: What to Check

No hot water, a cold burst in the shower or an error code on the display each point to a short list of causes. Start here.

A tankless water heater that stops working usually has one of a handful of problems. The usual suspects are no power, no gas, a blocked vent and scale inside the heat exchanger. A flow that is too low to trigger the burner, or a fault the unit shows as a code, round out the list. Several of these you can check in ten minutes without tools. Others, such as gas valve faults and cracked heat exchangers, need a licensed plumber.

This guide walks through the checks in a safe order, explains what the common symptoms mean and shows where a homeowner should stop. If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you do anything else.

If you smell gas, stop here

Gas-fired tankless units burn natural gas, so a leak is a real risk. Do not light anything, flip switches or try to find the leak. Get everyone out first.

  • Leave the building immediately and take other people and pets with you.
  • From outside or a neighbor's house, call 911 or the gas utility's 24-hour emergency line.
  • Do not use phones, light switches or doorbells inside until a utility crew says it is safe.
  • Call a plumber only after the utility clears the building.

Symptoms and what they usually mean

Match what you see to the likely cause. The last column shows whether a homeowner check is reasonable.

Common tankless water heater symptoms, likely causes and who handles them
SymptomLikely causeYour next step
No hot water, display is darkNo power or a tripped breaker or GFCICheck the outlet and breaker
Display lit, no hot waterGas valve closed, gas off or a fault codeCheck the gas valve, read the code
Water goes cold for a few secondsLow flow, scale or a short cycleOpen a second tap, then clean the filter
Water is lukewarm onlyScale on the heat exchanger or low gasPlumber, to flush the unit and test gas pressure

Unit clicks but never lightsIgniter, flame sensor or gas supplyPlumber

The manual usually lists every code with a one-line meaning. Keep it near the unit. If you have lost it, the model number on the label lets you find it online.

Safe checks in a sensible order

Work through these in sequence. Each step takes a few minutes.

01

Check power

Look for a lit display, then test the outlet and the GFCI. Reset the breaker if it has tripped.

02

Check the water supply

Confirm the cold inlet valve is fully open and other taps are running normally.

03

Check the gas valve

Confirm the handle is parallel to the pipe, which is open. Do not touch anything if you smell gas.

04

Read the code

Write down the number or letters from the display and compare with the manual.

05

Look at the vent and condensate line

Check outside for ice, snow or a nest at the vent termination. A condensing unit also drains water through a small tube that can freeze.

If checks one through four look fine and the code points to flame or gas, stop and call. Those parts are not do-it-yourself.

The cold water burst in the middle of a shower

Many tankless owners notice a cold slug of water between uses. The unit shuts off when flow drops below its minimum, and it needs a moment to ignite again when you open the tap. The water already in the pipe between the heater and the fixture is also cold, which can feel like a failure when it is not.

If the cold burst gets longer or happens mid-shower, check the faucet aerator for grit, open one tap fully instead of two partly and look at the inlet filter on the heater. A restricted filter lowers flow and can drop it below the trigger point. Scale is the next suspect, and a plumber can flush it.

Scale and your tap water

Mineral scale builds on the heat exchanger of any water heater, and the narrow passages in a tankless unit are quick to show it. Symptoms include lukewarm output, longer warm-up and error codes about temperature or flow.

Blue Island buys its water from Chicago, and the city's 2025 water quality report does not list hardness. That leaves your own tap as the better test. A test strip tells you whether to flush the unit once a year or more often. A plumber can run a descaling flush through the heat exchanger and clean the filter during the same visit.

Winter problems: frozen vents and condensate lines

Chicago-area January lows average 18.8 degrees Fahrenheit according to National Weather Service normals for O'Hare, and February averages 21.8. Cold like that can freeze the small condensate line on a high-efficiency tankless unit, trigger a code and shut the burner down. A vent that ends near snow drifts may also be blocked from outside.

Look outside for ice or a snowdrift against the vent. Clear it by hand if you can reach it safely from the ground. If the condensate line is frozen inside the house, thaw it gently with warm air. Do not use an open flame.

What stays with the plumber

Some repairs are beyond a safe homeowner fix, and gas appliances are at the top of the list.

Gas valve and burner faults

The unit lights, then drops out, or never lights. These need combustion testing.

Heat exchanger problems

A cracked or clogged exchanger needs a pro and sometimes a replacement.

Vent repair

A damaged or poorly pitched vent can send exhaust into the house.

Scale flush and service

A pump, hoses and a descaling solution make this a quick job for a plumber.

Choosing between service and replacement

A tankless unit that needs a flush, a filter clean or a new sensor is worth repairing. One with a cracked heat exchanger on an older unit may be worth replacing. Questions homeowners ask

The unit needs a minimum flow to ignite, and the pipe between the heater and the tap holds cold water that must clear first. A longer cold gap points to a restricted filter or scale.

Follow the manufacturer's schedule. Hard water means more frequent flushing, and Blue Island's report does not list hardness, so a test strip on your own tap tells you more than a guess.

Codes vary by brand and model. The manual lists each one. Typical codes relate to ignition, vent, flow and sensors. Write the code down and call a plumber if it returns after a reset.

The unit, the vent and the condensate line can all be affected. Keep the vent clear, protect any condensate tube and ask a plumber about freeze protection if the heater is in a cold space.
