Do You Need a Permit to Install a Water Heater in Blue Island, IL?

The short answer is to ask the city before work starts. Here is what Blue Island's code says about plumbing permits and who applies for them.

Permit rules for water heater installation vary from town to town, and Blue Island's city page does not state a rule for water heaters in particular. The reliable answer is to call the Building Safety Department at City Hall, 13051 Greenwood Avenue, (open Monday to Friday, 7:30 am to 4 pm) and ask about your exact job.

What the city code does say is who applies when plumbing work needs a permit. Section 150.052 treats the plumber as the owner's agent, and the Plumbing Inspector checks the license first. That rule is the reason license verification matters, and this guide explains how both pieces fit together.

What we can and cannot say about water heater permits

We reviewed Blue Island's Building Safety page and its code sections on permits. Section 150.045 requires a permit before a building is erected, altered or repaired. Section 150.052 requires permits for plumbing installation and repair. Neither section mentions water heaters by name.

That means we cannot tell you a permit is required, and we cannot tell you it is not. Call Building Safety, describe the heater you plan to install and ask what the city needs. Write down the date, the name of the person you spoke with and the answer.

How the permit process works under section 150.052

When plumbing work does need a permit in Blue Island, the code sets out the order of events.

01

Application at the Building Department

The plumber applies as the owner's agent. The Plumbing Inspector verifies a valid plumbing license or confirms that the applicant is the owner-occupant of a single-family residence.

02

Permit goes to Water and Sewer

The permit is presented to the Department of Water and Sewer before work begins.

03

Work proceeds

The contractor installs the heater according to the plumbing code the city follows.

04

Call before covering pipes

The contractor must call the Plumbing Inspector before any pipes are covered.

05

Final compliance

Final tap and water start happen only after the work complies.

The city lists the 2014 Illinois Plumbing Code among the codes it follows. The state code itself is 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890.

Why license verification matters

The Illinois Plumbing License Law, 225 ILCS 320, says a municipality may not issue a plumbing permit without verification that the applicant has a valid plumbing license. Blue Island's section 150.052 follows that rule, with an exception for owner-occupants of single-family homes. In practice, a permit and a licensed plumber go together.

You can check any plumber's license on the Plumbing License Verification page run by the Illinois Department of Public Health. The page we reviewed lists plumbers and says contractors register with the state. We did not confirm that the lookup covers contractor licenses, so ask the plumber for both the personal license and the contractor registration.

What Blue Island asks of plumbing contractors

The city also keeps its own contractor registration. Knowing the rules helps you sort serious contractors from the rest.

  • 1Registration with the city. Ordinance 99-247 says all contractors must register to do business in Blue Island.
  • 2State licenses on file. Plumbing contractors need a current Illinois Department of Public Health license, and the city asks for both the 055 and 058 licenses.
  • 3Insurance minimums. General liability of $1,000,000 per occurrence plus statutory workers compensation, under section 118.019.
  • 4Fee and bond. Plumbing contractors are exempt from the local fee and from the surety bond.

Ask a plumber to show proof of registration and insurance. A contractor who has both will not mind the question.

What happens if no one pulls a permit

We found no published penalty in the sources we reviewed, so we will not guess. The practical risks are easier to describe. An unpermitted job may not be inspected, a future buyer's inspector may ask about it and the city has a rule that houses be inspected before occupancy under Ordinance 09-048.

That rule matters for sellers and landlords. If you plan to sell or rent a unit, call Building Safety and ask what documentation the inspection will expect for a recently installed heater.
