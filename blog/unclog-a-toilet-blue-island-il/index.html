How to Unclog a Toilet in Blue Island, IL

A rising bowl is stressful, but most clogs clear in minutes with the right plunger and the right motion. Here is what to do, in order, and where to stop.

To unclog a toilet, stop flushing, lift the tank lid and close the flapper if the bowl is rising, then seat a flange plunger so the rubber covers the drain hole completely. Push down gently once to release trapped air, then pump with firm, short strokes for 15 to 20 seconds. If the water drains in a swirl, the clog is gone.

Most people make the clog worse by flushing again to see what happens. A second flush sends more water into a bowl that cannot drain, and the overflow lands on your floor. Work through the steps below in order, and you will know within about ten minutes whether this is a plunger job or a plumber job.

The bowl is rising right now: do this first

Water on the way to the rim can be stopped in seconds. Take the tank lid off and push the rubber flapper down into its seat so no more water enters the bowl. If the flapper will not hold, turn the shutoff valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.

  • Do not flush again, even if the bowl looks like it is draining slowly.
  • Close the flapper or the shutoff valve behind the toilet to stop the refill.
  • Lay towels around the base and keep children and pets out of the bathroom.
  • Stay away from wet outlets and floor heaters if water has reached the floor.

If water is also coming up in the tub or shower, stop here. That points to the main drain, not the toilet, and a plunger will not fix it. Call (773) 207-0518 and a licensed plumber can come out on the same day when appointments are open, or at night through the 24/7 emergency line.

The plunger method, step by step

A flange plunger, the kind with a smaller rubber sleeve folded inside the cup, fits a toilet drain far better than the flat sink plunger. If your plunger is flat, it can still work, but it takes more effort to get a seal.

01

Add water if the bowl is low

The cup needs to sit under water to push it. Pour in a bucket of warm tap water until the bowl is about half full, without letting it spill.

02

Seat the plunger

Tilt it so air escapes as it goes in, then press the rubber sleeve into the drain opening until the cup flattens against the porcelain.

03

Push gently, pull sharply

The first push forces air into the trap, so go easy. The pull stroke does most of the work because it tugs the clog loose.

04

Pump 10 to 15 times

Keep the seal and repeat in a steady rhythm. Listen for a gurgle and watch for the level to drop.

05

Test with a small amount of water

Pour a half bucket into the bowl. If it drains normally, wait a minute, then flush once.

If the water level does not move after three rounds of plunging, you are probably dealing with something firmer than paper. Toys, wipes, cotton products and thick wads of paper often need an auger instead.

Safe backups if the plunger is not enough

Before you reach for a chemical, try the gentler options. They solve a good share of stubborn clogs without risk to your pipes or your skin.

  • 1Hot water and dish soap. Squirt a generous amount of dish soap into the bowl, then pour in a bucket of hot (not boiling) tap water from waist height. Boiling water can crack porcelain, so skip it.
  • 2Toilet auger. A closet auger has a curved shaft that protects the porcelain. Feed the cable in, crank slowly, and retrieve or break up the blockage.
  • 3Wet and dry vacuum. A shop vacuum set to wet mode can pull a clog out of the trap. Seal the hose around the drain and be ready for a messy cleanup.
  • 4Skip drain chemicals. Caustic cleaners sit in a blocked bowl and can splash on skin. They can also hurt older fittings and make life harder for the plumber who arrives next.

When to stop and call a plumber

A bowl that will not clear after a fair try is not a failure on your part. It tells you the blockage is further down.

The auger will not pass

Resistance deep in the line can mean a lodged object or a collapsed section, and forcing the cable can scratch the bowl.

Several fixtures are slow

When the toilet, shower and kitchen sink all drain badly, the main drain is the likelier culprit.

Sewage smell or standing sewage

Treat it as a health hazard. Keep everyone out of the area and avoid touching the water.

Repeated clogs

Two or three clogs in a month point to a line problem, an old toilet with a weak flush, or roots in the line outside.

A plumber can run a drain machine through the line or send a camera down to see what is blocking it. If the problem is in the house sewer itself, the fix may involve drain cleaning or hydro jetting rather than anything at the toilet.

Why a toilet clogs in the first place

Most toilets clog from too much paper at once or from items that were never meant to be flushed. Wipes labeled flushable still tangle in the trap, and they snag on roughness in older cast iron pipe. Paper towels and feminine products do not break down the way toilet paper does.

Older low-flow models from the 1990s sometimes flush with too little force to carry waste through the whole line. If a toilet clogs every few weeks even with careful use, the problem may be the fixture itself. In that case a toilet repair or replacement visit makes more sense than repeated plunging.

A toilet clog that also slows a tub points toward the house sewer. Section 52.009 of Blue Island's code describes those sewers as tile or cast iron, at least 6 inches across, so an aged, rough pipe is a likely suspect.
