A tankless water heater warms water only while a tap runs, so there is no tank to leak and no standing reserve to run out. That also means the unit lives or dies by flow rate, gas supply, venting and mineral buildup. How a tankless heater works, and who it suits

When you open a hot tap, a flow sensor tells the unit to fire or energize. Water passes through a heat exchanger and leaves at the set temperature. Stop the flow, and the unit shuts off.

The limit is the flow rate at a given temperature rise. In January the Chicago area averages a low of 18.8 F at O'Hare (NWS 1991 to 2020 normals), and the incoming water is cold as well. That larger rise means a unit rated for a certain flow in summer delivers less in winter.

Tankless suits households that value space and steady hot water. It suits a house with several people less well than a home with two showers that rarely overlap, unless the unit is sized for peak demand.

Installing a tankless unit, in sequence

This is more involved than a tank swap, which is why we walk the house first.

01

Load and flow calculation

We total the fixtures that can run together and compare them with the unit's rated flow at your winter water temperature.

02

03

The unit mounts on the wall. Isolation valves go on both water lines so it can be flushed later, and a condensate drain is installed where required.

We fire the unit, check combustion, test temperature at several fixtures and show you the controls and the error code display.

Older houses, gas lines and exterior vent terminations

About 64.7% of Blue Island housing was built before 1970. In those homes, the gas pipe serving the old tank may be too small for a tankless burner, so replacing a stretch of pipe can be part of the job.

Exterior vents also matter. The city requires a Certificate of Appropriateness for permits on landmarked buildings and in the Olde Western Avenue Historic District, so if your building sits in or near it, call Building Safety at City Hall, 13051 Greenwood Avenue, before choosing a side-wall vent location.

Tankless repair: what usually goes wrong

Most service calls fall into a few categories, and the display code usually points to one.

Error codes

Codes can indicate ignition, flame sensing, exhaust or temperature faults. Write the code down before you reset the unit.

Cold-water sandwich

A burst of cold water between two hot ones usually relates to short, repeated draws or a sensor issue.

Low flow

A clogged inlet filter screen or scale in the heat exchanger cuts flow and triggers shutdowns.

Leaks

Leaks at connections or the cabinet may be a fitting, or the heat exchanger. We find the source before we recommend replacement.

Service depends on the model and parts access. Tell us the make and model when you call so we know what to bring.

Flushing and descaling the heat exchanger

Minerals in the water slowly coat the inside of the heat exchanger, and a coated exchanger heats less efficiently and runs hotter. A flush removes that scale before it hardens.

The process uses the isolation valves. We close the water, connect a small pump and hoses, and circulate a descaling solution through the unit, then rinse it clean. We also clean the inlet filter screen.

Blue Island buys Lake Michigan water from Chicago, but the city's published water reports do not list a hardness figure. Test your tap with a hardness strip or ask the Blue Island Water and Sewer Department. Then follow the manufacturer's flushing interval for your unit, and shorten it if the water proves hard.

Every tankless unit ships with manufacturer installation instructions covering venting, gas line size and clearances, and the install follows them along with the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890). Whether a heater swap needs a city permit is a question for Building Safety, so ask before the install date.

Signs a tankless unit needs attention

Catch these early and you may only need a flush.

  • 1Hot water takes longer to arrive than it used to.
  • 2The temperature swings or turns lukewarm at high flow.
  • 3An error code returns after you reset the unit.
  • 4You hear rumbling or kettle-like noise while it runs.
  • 5Water or rust shows at the isolation valves or cabinet.
  • 6The unit has never been flushed since it was installed.
