Emergency drain cleaning: what to do right now

If water is rising in a tub, a floor drain or a toilet bowl, treat it as an emergency. Keep people and pets out of standing water near outlets, extension cords, the furnace and the water heater. If it is sewage, wear boots and gloves and skip the wet vacuum.

  • Stop all water use. No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher until the line is open.
  • Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor before the water spreads.
  • Leave bottled drain chemicals on the shelf. They sit in the pipe and can burn skin when a plumber opens it.
  • Note which fixtures are slow or gurgling so you can tell the dispatcher when you call (773) 207-0518.

After-hours calls go to the 24/7 emergency line, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. Tell us whether one drain or several are affected, because that decides which machine rides on the truck.

Which drain is clogged tells us where to start

The same cable does not suit every pipe. Where the water backs up points to how far down the blockage sits.

Kitchen sink

Cooking grease cools into a paste on the pipe wall and catches rice, coffee grounds and eggshells. A sink that drains in slow motion for weeks has usually narrowed to a thin channel. A small sink machine with a cutting head scrapes the wall clean instead of just punching through.

Shower or tub

Hair wraps around the stopper and the first bend of the trap, then binds with soap film. Check the sink in the same bathroom, since they often share a branch. We pull the debris out rather than push it deeper.

Washer line

Lint and detergent residue build up in the standpipe and the branch behind it. If the washer drain overflows the box, the blockage may sit past the standpipe, and the cable has to go further.

Main line

When toilets gurgle, a basement floor drain bubbles and several fixtures slow together, the blockage is on the shared line. That calls for a heavier sectional cable or a jetter.

How a clogged drain gets cleared

The job follows the same order every time, so we are not guessing at a pipe we cannot see.

01

Find the blockage

We test fixtures, listen for gurgling and locate the nearest access, such as a trap, a roof vent or the main cleanout.

02

Run the cable

A drum or sectional machine feeds a spring cable into the pipe. The size of the machine matches the size of the line.

03

Cut and retrieve

A cutting head slices roots and scale, while a retrieving head hooks hair and rags. We flush with water to push remnants out.

04

Confirm the line

We run water through every fixture on that branch. If it still drags, a camera shows whether the pipe is cracked or sagging.

Older pipe and shared lines in Blue Island

About 64.7% of Blue Island housing was built before 1970, and the median build year is 1961 (CMAP, 2020 to 2024 survey data). Homes from that era often have cast iron or clay tile drains with rough, scaled walls that catch debris. City code calls for a house sewer of at least 6 inches inside, tile or cast iron, so a cable needs room to pass bends.

Nearly half of homes are rented (48.2%), and many buildings share one drain line across several units. If you rent, tell your landlord the moment a second unit reports a backup.

Most of the city also drains through combined sewers, so if every drain slows during a heavy storm, the cause may sit in the street sewer. In that case ask the Blue Island Water and Sewer Department before you pay for a cable.

One fixture or the whole house? Signs of a main line clog

A single slow sink is a branch problem. These signs point to the main drain.

  • 1Several fixtures slow at once. A tub and a toilet on different floors both drain badly.
  • 2Toilets gurgle when another fixture runs. Air is being pushed past a blockage.
  • 3Floor drain smells or bubbles. The basement drain is the lowest point and shows trouble first.
  • 4Water appears in a tub after laundry. Wastewater is finding the nearest open path.
  • 5A sewer gas smell with no obvious source. A dry trap or a leaking joint can cause it.

When a clog hides a leaking drain line

Sometimes the blockage is not the whole story. A cracked or separated drain line leaks into the soil or crawlspace, and a damp spot, a stain below a bathroom or a lingering sewer smell can show up before the water stops flowing.

We run a camera when a clog returns or a smell lingers, then explain whether it is a cleaning job or a sewer line repair. You see the same picture we do.

Blue Island code section 52.009 sets house sewers at tile or cast iron, not less than 6 inches inside diameter. That gives a technician a known pipe size to work with, though an old line may be rougher or narrower inside. A repair that opens the pipe falls under the permit rule in section 150.052.
