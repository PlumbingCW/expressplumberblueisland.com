Signs the tank is finished, not just tired

Replacement is the right call when the vessel itself has failed. Parts fix components, but they cannot restore rusted steel.

  • 1Water seeps from the tank shell. A wet seam, a rust trail down the side or a dripping jacket means the inner tank has corroded through.
  • 2Rusty hot water. Brown water from hot taps only, with clear cold water, points at the tank interior.
  • 3Repeated repairs. When the second or third part fails in a short span, further repairs rarely pay off.
  • 4Heavy sediment and noise. A floor of hardened scale that a flush cannot clear shortens the tank's life and wastes fuel.
  • 5An old rating plate. The date code on the plate tells you the age. Many callers do not know it until we read it.

Tank failed overnight? What to do until we arrive

Stop the water, stop the fuel, and protect the floor. A full tank holds a lot of water, and a failing one can empty into a finished basement quickly.

Our 24/7 emergency line takes this call at any hour. Tell us the fuel type and tank location, and we will line up the swap.

  • Close the cold-water valve on the pipe feeding the tank.
  • Switch off the breaker (electric) or turn the gas control to off (gas).
  • Move stored items off the floor and stay clear of wet outlets.
  • Gas smell? Get everyone outside and call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line first.

If the tank is only leaking slowly, you may have a few hours to compare options. Ask about same-day appointments when available, and plan to be home so we can reach the water shutoff and the heater.

Tank, hybrid or tankless: how the choices compare

A failed tank in a cold basement is the moment to reconsider the whole setup. Chicago-area winter air temperatures average a January low of 18.8 F at O'Hare, and incoming water is cold too, which affects every type below.

Trade-offs between the three common replacement types
TypeStrengthsTrade-offs
Conventional tank, gas or electricLowest purchase price, simple swap, easy partsStandby heat loss, a limited hot supply, and a tank to fail again
Hybrid heat pump tankUses much less electricity than a standard electric tankNeeds air space and a condensate drain, runs noisy, costs more upfront
TanklessEndless hot water at the flow it can handle, wall-mountedOften needs a larger gas line, new venting and periodic descaling

If tankless sounds right, read the details on our tankless water heater page. We will not push it, because a like-for-like tank is often the better move for a rushed swap.

Sizing the new heater to the people in the house

The label that matters is the first-hour rating, which tells you how much hot water the unit delivers in a busy hour. Tank capacity alone is misleading. Count how many showers, loads of laundry and dishwasher cycles overlap on your busiest morning.

The average Blue Island household has 2.56 people, but your own count is what counts. A family with teenagers needs more delivery than a couple, even in the same house.

A bigger tank costs more to heat and to buy. Too small a tank runs out mid-shower. We size to your real pattern, then check that the unit fits through the basement stair and under the ductwork.

Replacement day, from drain-down to first hot tap

A standard swap runs in this order, and we explain each step as it happens.

01

Shut off and drain

We close the water and fuel, attach a hose to the drain valve and empty the old tank to a floor drain or outside.

02

Remove and prepare

The old unit comes out. We inspect the shutoff, the gas or electric connection, the venting and the floor pan.

03

Set and connect

The new unit goes in on a level base. New flexible connectors, a relief valve and a discharge pipe are fitted, plus an expansion tank where the setup calls for one.

04

Start up and test

We fill the tank, purge air, test for leaks, light or power the unit and check flame, draft and temperature at a tap.

Venting basics for a gas replacement

A gas water heater must send its exhaust outdoors. Older tanks use natural draft and a flue that rises through a chimney, while newer ones may use a fan to push exhaust through plastic pipe out a side wall.

Roughly 64.7% of Blue Island housing predates 1970, and many of those homes have masonry chimneys. A new unit connected to an old, oversized chimney can draft poorly or let moisture collect, so a liner or a switch to power venting may be part of the job. Callers often ask to reroute an old chimney vent out the side of the house, and we can check whether that fits your layout.

Install a carbon monoxide alarm near the heater and the bedrooms. If an alarm sounds, get outside and call 911.

Permits, licences and who to call at City Hall

Whether your water heater swap needs a permit is a question for the City of Blue Island Building Safety Department at City Hall, 13051 Greenwood Avenue. Call them before the work date and ask what applies to a like-for-like replacement and to any change in fuel or venting.

Verify any plumber's licence yourself on the Illinois Department of Public Health Plumbing License Verification lookup. We are licensed and insured, and we will tell you what the city says before the work starts.

Plumbing work in Illinois falls under the Illinois Plumbing License Law (225 ILCS 320), and Blue Island's section 150.052 requires a plumbing permit for installation and repair. The city's pages do not say whether a water heater swap specifically needs one, so call Building Safety at City Hall before the day of the work and ask.
